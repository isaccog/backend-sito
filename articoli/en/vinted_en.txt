titolo: A Note on a Trade War
pubblicazione: 2025-12-12
testo:
In France, Vinted is the leading online clothing seller, with Shein following behind. The clash is amusing: a Lithuanian startup selling second-hand clothes against a Chinese multinational that generates garments with AI, mass-produces them, and throws away millions if they aren't rewarded by the algorithm.
A hasty reading pits reuse against waste. Someone then notices that the consumerist hunger on Vinted is certainly no smaller, and that we'll need to see how much pollution is caused by sending all these packages from one individual to another.
Either way, that's not enough to shed light on the matter. The most interesting thing is to see the difference between the old industrial capitalism (paradoxically: that of the new Chinese power, which in fact struggles to truly become one) and the service capitalism now prevalent in old Europe.
Shein's sophisticated techniques for optimizing everything are actually rather clumsy at bottom: they increase production speed, optimize cost, but don't change the direction.

Vinted's resounding success lies in completely freeing the clothing sector from its physical substance. Consumers themselves become sellers and producers. The company handles two things, one central, one ancillary.
The ancillary one is making the process smooth: shipping, receiving, etc. The central one is building desire, creating the space to bring consumption to life. And skimming off a fraction of it, calling it purchase protection.
Vinted is an insurance company on your ongoing capacity to regenerate pleasure in buying and gratification in selling.
The ancillary activity exists to let you live the central one without hassle: the pornographic pleasure of scrolling and buying.

In the end, Vinted doesn't become a subject of these transactions, but the ground on which they happen. If Shein tries to avoid the responsibilities tied to its actions (waste, pollution, exploitation, etc.), Vinted pulls off the reverse trick: it doesn't need to avoid them, because it doesn't have any.
The responsibility — for hyper-production, hyper-consumption, quality — is yours.

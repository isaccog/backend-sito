titolo: Ice Cream Is Good but It Costs Millions
pubblicazione: 2026-07-24
testo:
We were having granita one evening in Milan, and beyond the (positive) reviews, it's undeniable that we let ourselves be won over by the aesthetics. The ice cream in the well, the ice cream kept out of sight, was an implicit suggestion of 'artisanal' quality.
Except the granita turned out to be a sort of melted Big Babol bubblegum. Sugary, almost rubbery.

Every apostate of purity, of tradition, of the local slips on ice cream. Everyone we despise slips on ice cream, and so we eat a lot of it just to maximize the odds of their fall.

Ice cream doesn't exist as a traditional product before the arrival of stabilizers and the refrigerator. Up until the 1900s we're talking about more or less creamy snow, which today we'd consider a badly made granita.
Ice cream therefore requires chemical products (or uncommon natural ingredients like carob flour) to exist.

Ice cream isn't artisanal. Take a chef and call them to your home. They'll make you a Michelin-starred dinner. Call a gelato maker to your home: they'll manage very little. Ice cream, even when artisanal, requires industrial machinery.

Ice cream isn't fresh, even though it's cold. What sense does it make to talk about fresh ingredients in ice cream? Who gives up strawberry when strawberries are only in season for two months a year?

Ice cream isn't local: vanilla, cocoa, pistachio, etc. The most beloved flavors incorporate the least local ingredients.

When I was a kid, most gelato shops had very colorful flavors (Smurf blue!), visible and sugary. Now the 'good' gelato shops hide the flavors in the well to remind us that they don't dress up their ice cream. That they use only natural ingredients.
Only local ingredients.
